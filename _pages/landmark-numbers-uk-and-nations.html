Many UK statistics are really English statistics, and many policies (health, education, transport) are run separately in each nation. Knowing how the four nations compare helps you spot when a figure covers only part of the UK, and whether a difference between them is big or small.

Reading these numbers
  • Check which nation a figure covers. Many "UK" statistics, including most NHS figures, cover England only. With {{ eng_share }}% of the population, England dominates any UK total.
  • The periods differ. Each figure says which period it covers. Compare like with like before drawing conclusions.
  • Where the figures come from. Every figure is numbered and listed at the end of its section, with a link to the official source: mostly the ONS, government departments and the devolved governments. Shares, rates and per-person figures are worked out from the figures shown. I check for newer releases every quarter and update the pages through a reviewed change.

This is my personal site, so these are my reading of public data.
