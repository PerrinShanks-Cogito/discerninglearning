Many UK statistics are really English statistics, and many policies (health, education, transport) are run separately in each nation. Knowing how the four nations compare helps you spot when a figure covers only part of the UK, and whether a difference between them is big or small.

Reading these numbers

  • Check which nation a figure covers. Many "UK" statistics, including most NHS figures, cover England only. With {{ eng_share }}% of the population, England dominates any UK total.
  • Spending per person reflects need as well as choices. The devolved governments are funded largely through a block grant (set using the Barnett formula), and older, more rural or poorer populations cost more to serve. Higher spending per person is not by itself a sign of waste or generosity.
  • GDP per person can mislead for small economies. It counts output where it is produced, so commuting, company headquarters and North Sea oil (allocated to the UK, not to Scotland) all affect it. Median pay is a better guide to what people earn.
  • Healthy life expectancy is based on how people rate their own health, so it is less precise than life expectancy, especially for the smaller nations.
  • The periods differ. Population is mid-2025, spending is 2024/25, employment is mid-2026. Compare like with like before drawing conclusions.
Where these figures come from

Every row links to its source: the Office for National Statistics (population, land area, life expectancy, healthy life expectancy, GDP, pay and employment) and HM Treasury's Country and regional analysis (public spending). Median pay comes from the ONS Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings through its Nomis service. People per km², the index and the shares are worked out from the figures shown. I check for newer releases every quarter and update the page through a reviewed change.
