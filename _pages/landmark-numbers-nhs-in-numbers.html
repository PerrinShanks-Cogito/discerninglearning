The NHS is too big to picture, which makes almost any number about it sound large. These figures are for the NHS in England. The per-day and per-person versions are the ones worth remembering: they turn a headline into something you can compare.

Rules of thumb

Turn any NHS number into a per-day, per-person or per-member-of-staff figure before deciding whether it is big.

  • Divide by 365. NHS England spends about £{{ per_day_m }} million a day, so a £50 million announcement is about {{ announce_hours }} hours of NHS running costs.
  • Divide by the population. With about {{ pop | divided_by: 1000000 | round: 1 }} million people, every £1 billion is about £{{ 1000000000 | divided_by: pop | round }} per person.
  • Divide by the workforce. With about {{ wf.nurses_midwives | divided_by: 1000 }},000 nurses and midwives, "10,000 extra nurses" is about {{ nurse_pct }}% more.
  • Count pathways or people? The waiting list counts treatments, not patients. Check which one a headline uses before comparing it with anything else.
  • Check the period. A monthly figure, a yearly figure and a financial-year figure can't be compared directly, and months vary with the seasons.
Where these figures come from

Each card links to its source. Activity and cost figures come from NHS England's National Cost Collection and update automatically when a new edition is published. The budget, population, workforce, GP appointment and waiting list figures are updated after a quarterly check for newer releases. The per-day, per-person and "1 in" figures are worked out from the figures shown, so they change with them.
