Notes on these figures
  • Spending per person reflects need as well as choices. The devolved governments are funded largely through a block grant (set using the Barnett formula), and older, more rural or poorer populations cost more to serve. Higher spending per person is not by itself a sign of waste or generosity.
  • GDP per person can mislead for small economies. It counts output where it is produced, so commuting, company headquarters and North Sea oil (allocated to the UK, not to Scotland) all affect it. Median pay is a better guide to what people earn.
  • There is no single count of "people on benefits". The claimant count covers people who claim because they are unemployed. Universal Credit is wider, and the State Pension and disability benefits are separate again. Social protection spending covers all of these, plus social care.
